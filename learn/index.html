What are you stuck on right now?

Most people land here mid-panic: the sample your supervisor just questioned, the references that broke the night before the deadline, the reviewer comment you can’t decode. Pick the moment. Get one clear next step, and the sentence you can show your supervisor.

The research journey

Find where you are stuck. Each stage is one decision and the guides that settle it.

I can’t turn my idea into a question

  • Find your next research projectMap the field, check which gaps are real, and choose the project that survives checking.

My data won’t load, or isn’t clean enough to analyse

You cannot run a single test until the data is clean, structured, and de-identified. Do this first.

  • Clean and prepare your data, in R and PythonThe whole workflow on one page: missing values, types, validation, duplicates, merges, and de-identifying before you share.
  • Build a data dictionary that validatesDefine variables, types, allowed values, and validation before you collect.
  • My survey export has two header rowsCombine the headers and reshape a SurveyMonkey or Qualtrics export for R and Python.
  • My numbers turned to text in the CSVDecimal commas, semicolons, and locale, and how to import cleanly.
4

I don’t understand my own statistics

Read the few things that change your conclusion, in plain words.

  • What do my results actually say?Effect size, significance, and the confidence interval — the four things that change the meaning, no equations.
5

This review is drowning me — when do I stop?

Set a defensible stopping rule, and screen a slice at a time.

  • When is my systematic search finished?A defensible stopping rule, screening in slices, and PRISMA counts as you go.

Can I collect this without breaking the rules?

Keep sensitive data on your machine, and anonymise it properly.

  • Transcribe interviews locally with WhisperInstall, choose a model, transcribe, and keep the recording on your own computer.
  • Build a qualitative codebookOne note per code: definition, inclusion, exclusion, anchor quote, revision history.

  • I have coded my data. How do I find the themes?Codes are not themes: the descriptive-to-analytic move, with a worked example and a trail.
  • Local AI coding, with human reviewThe model suggests first-cycle codes; you review every one and record the decision.
  • What LLMs can do, and where they stopWhere models help, five ways their output is over-trusted, and a workable division of labour.

8

I’m staring at a wall and can’t write

Beat the blank page with structure, not willpower.

  • Agile writing. Four decisions that finish a paperA stalled paper is usually stuck on one decision. Find yours with a free self-check.
  • Write from the results outFigures and methods first, introduction near the end, abstract last.
  • My figures are “too low-resolution”. What do they want?Formats, DPI, font sizes, and colour-blind-safe colour, with a pre-submission checklist.
  • Can every reader tell my figure colours apart?Build a palette from one colour and test it under simulated colour-blindness, in Python or R.
  • My bibliography is broken. How do I recover it?Recover a corrupted Zotero or EndNote document, then stop it recurring.
  • Zotero for researchersCollect, organise, annotate, and cite, plus FullVahti and Better BibTeX.
  • Make your analysis code citableGive the version you actually ran a release, a DOI, and a code-availability statement.
9

The reviewers came back and I don’t know what they want

Triage the comments, decode the feedback, keep it polite.

  • Triage a major-revisions letterProcess the hit, sort the comments, and handle two reviewers who contradict.
  • Respond without losing a commentGive every request a stance, response, real change, and location.
  • What does ‘more depth’ actually mean?The five vague supervisor comments, translated into concrete fixes.
  • Every research project needs a CHANGELOGAnswer “why did you change that?” from a dated entry instead of memory.
10

I submitted and now they want a huge fee

Check the red flags, and know your options now.

  • Is this journal predatory, or just expensive?The red-flag pattern, what to do if you’ve already submitted, and no-fee routes to open access.
