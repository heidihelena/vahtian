The statement is the easy part

Six months of AI use. One empty disclosure box.

Submission asks what you used, which version, where it touched the work, and what you checked. Your chat history is not a research record.

Most AI disclosures are written backwards: first the polished sentence, then a hurried attempt to remember what actually happened. That is how a useful record turns into “ChatGPT was used for editing.”

The better habit is small: one line when the work happens. At submission, filter the lines that matter and write the statement from them.

Record the tool you actually used

“Claude” or “ChatGPT” is not quite enough. Keep the provider, model or version, access route, date, task, affected material, and your own check. If a service does not expose a stable version number, record the model label and date accessed.

Example record · one line per work session

2026-03-04 | Anthropic Claude | Sonnet 4 | web app
LANG | Methods 2.3 | language edit | reviewed line by line; 2 sentences kept

2026-03-11 | whisper.cpp 1.7.5 | Whisper large-v3 | local
TRANS | interviews 4-9 | first-pass transcript | full audio-text check completed 2026-03-12

2026-04-02 | OpenAI ChatGPT | GPT-4.1 | web app
IDEA | Discussion outline | counterarguments | no generated text reused
DateWhen the use happened, not when you remembered it.
Tool + providerThe product or application and who supplies it.
Model or versionThe model label, software version, or both.
Access routeWeb app, API, institutional deployment, or local tool.
Task + scopeWhat it did and which section, file, dataset, or interview it touched.
Human actionWhat you kept, changed, rejected, tested, or checked against the source.

Started late?

Start with today. Reconstruct earlier use where you can, label those entries as reconstructed, and do not invent precision you do not have. A partial record that says what is known is more useful than a confident paragraph built from guesswork.
