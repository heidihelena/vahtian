The journal wants an AI statement. You used AI months ago.

Most disclosure statements get written from memory the night before submission — vague, nervous, and one venue-policy detail away from wrong. The fix is not better memory. It is a log you keep while you work.

This is the whole mechanism:

2026-03-04 · Claude (Sonnet) · language editing, Methods §2.3 · output reviewed, 2 sentences kept
2026-03-11 · Whisper large-v3, local · transcription, interviews 4-9 · full human pass 03-12
2026-04-02 · ChatGPT · brainstormed discussion counterarguments · no text reused

Tool, version, what it touched, the date, and what a human did about it. One line at the time beats a paragraph reconstructed six months later, and when the venue asks, the statement is a filtering exercise instead of an act of recall. Policies genuinely conflict — some publishers want tool, version, manufacturer, and dates; some exempt copy-editing entirely — so the log holds everything and the venue's own instructions decide what goes in.

Started late? Start anyway.

A log begun today covers you from today forward. For the months before it, reconstruct what you can honestly, mark it as reconstructed, and let the dated part of the record carry the weight. That is still a stronger statement than memory alone.
