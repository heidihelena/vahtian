Thirteen comments, two reviewers, and the buried one gets missed

Some comments arrive numbered. Others sit three lines deep in a paragraph of prose. Editors cross-check the response letter against both, and the missed one costs you a round.

A response letter is judged on two things you can actually check before submitting: coverage and honesty. Three failure modes account for most of the damage:

The buried comment.

It was never numbered, so it never made it into your letter. The reviewer notices its absence first.

A claim with no change.

"We have revised accordingly" with nothing findable in the manuscript. Editors look for the location.

A disagreement with no reason.

Declining a request is legitimate — reviewers and editors accept a grounded no. An unexplained no reads as an ignored comment.

The fix is a ledger: every comment on its own row, with a stance, a response, the real change, and where in the manuscript it lives. Writing the letter then becomes assembly, and checking coverage becomes reading a table instead of re-reading two reviews at midnight.
