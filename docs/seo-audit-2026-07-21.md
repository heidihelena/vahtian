# vahtian.com — Technical SEO, Search Quality & Information Architecture Audit

**Date:** 2026-07-21 · **Scope:** all 97 HTML pages in the repo (the deployed asset set)
· **Method:** full source crawl of the repo, link-graph reconstruction, metadata
extraction, structured-data parsing, plus the repo's own CI gate
(`.github/scripts/audit.sh`).

**Audit limitation (disclosed up front):** this audit ran in a sandboxed
environment whose egress policy blocks requests to `vahtian.com` and most external
domains. Everything below is verified against the deployed source tree; the small
set of live-only checks (www→apex redirect, HSTS header, trailing-slash redirect,
external-link liveness) could not be confirmed from here and is listed in
§1.9 as a 15-minute manual verification pass. Google Search Console data was not
available; §2 indexability states are therefore documented estimates, not GSC facts.

---

## Executive summary

This is one of the cleanest small sites I have audited. The hygiene layer that
consumes most audits elsewhere is essentially done: **zero duplicate titles, zero
duplicate descriptions, exactly one H1 on every one of 97 pages, zero missing alt
attributes, zero broken internal links, zero orphan pages, zero JSON-LD parse
errors, a perfectly consistent sitemap (every indexable page in, every noindex page
out), and a real 404** (`wrangler.jsonc` → `not_found_handling: "404-page"`, so no
soft-404 class exists). The site's own CI drift gate passes. Content quality is the
site's strongest asset by Google's Helpful Content criteria: question-phrased
titles that mirror real researcher queries, first-person experience from a named
MD/PhD author, original data (e.g. the 120-pair local-model citation test), and
honest scope limits ("agreement is not accuracy") that most competitors do not print.

What remains is a short list of real defects and a larger strategic layer:

1. **One page violates the house SEO standard** — `extractvahti/` (em-dash title
   without the `· Vahtian` suffix; 200-char description that will truncate).
2. **One canonical bug** — `qualivahti-local/success/` canonical missing its
   trailing slash (noindex page, so cosmetic, but it's drift).
3. **Sitemap `<lastmod>` is mass-bumped** — 88 of 89 URLs share `2026-07-19`,
   which teaches Google to ignore the site's lastmod signal.
4. **23 learn articles have machine-readable dates but no visible date**, and
   there is no standalone `Person` entity or `BreadcrumbList` schema anywhere.
5. **Fifteen indexable pages have ≤2 inbound internal links** — the cluster
   structure exists editorially but is under-expressed as links.
6. The biggest strategic opportunity is not fixing anything but **finishing the
   workflow-stage information architecture** the site already gestures at
   (`/download/` is organised by research stage; `/learn/` by problem) and
   filling ~8 high-intent content gaps that sit adjacent to existing tools.

Estimated overall state: **technical ~97/100, content ~90/100, architecture ~75/100,
EEAT expression ~70/100** (the expertise exists; the machine-readable and visible
signalling of it is incomplete).

### 1.2 Explicitly checked and clean

- **Duplicate titles / descriptions / H1s:** none (Counter over all 97 pages).
- **Missing / multiple H1s:** none — exactly one per page, including inside-JS
  template H1s correctly excluded.
- **Canonical presence:** every indexable page has one; the only pages without
  (`404.html`, `/p/*`, `/brand/carousel/`, `/thank-you/`) are all noindex, where a
  canonical is unnecessary.
- **Broken internal links:** none. (Five apparent hits were `href` inside JS
  template literals — not crawlable anchors.)
- **Orphan pages:** none — every indexable page has ≥1 inbound link and all are
  reachable from the homepage.
- **Crawl depth:** home=0; 27 pages at depth 1; 54 at depth 2; 3 at depth 3; 1 at
  depth 4 (`learn/obsidian-plugins-for-researchers/`). Excellent for 97 pages.
- **Redirect chains/loops:** none possible — no `_redirects` file; all internal
  hrefs point at final trailing-slash URLs.
- **Image alt:** 0 missing across all pages.
- **JS rendering:** all marketing/article content is static HTML; JS powers only
  the in-browser tools. Nothing indexable depends on JS execution.
- **robots.txt:** valid; allows all; explicitly welcomes AI crawlers (GPTBot,
  ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot); declares the sitemap.
- **Soft 404s:** structurally impossible — Cloudflare serves `404.html` with a
  real 404 status (`not_found_handling: "404-page"`).
- **Pagination:** none exists; `/learn/` lists all articles on one page — correct

- **URL consistency:** single scheme (apex, https, trailing slash) everywhere in
  hrefs and canonicals, minus finding #3.
- **noindex mistakes:** none — every noindex page *should* be noindex
  (purchase-success, share stubs, internal tooling).

## Phase 3 — Search intent audit

The title inventory (full dump in audit data) shows a deliberate and unusually
good pattern: **learn titles are the researcher's own question**, verbatim:

- "How do I justify my qualitative sample size?" — exact match for the real query
  *justify qualitative sample size*; H1 identical; intro opens with the supervisor
  scenario. **Deserves to outrank university methods pages** because it answers in
  the first screen, gives a worked example at n=20, and cites information power —
  most university pages define saturation and stop.
- "My numbers turned to text when I opened the CSV", "Is this journal predatory,
  or just expensive?", "I received major revisions. Where do I start?" — all
  query-shaped, all matching H1s.

Where intent-match is weaker:

| Page | Problem | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Product H1s: `ExtractVahti`, `GuidelineVahti`, `SynthVahti`, `ReviewVahti`, `QualiVahti Local`, `Research Strategy Kit`, `MatchVahti·Lite` | Bare product name carries zero query terms; contrast with the benefit-led H1s (`Check what the source actually supports.`, `Defend the sample you can actually recruit.`) used by the stronger pages. A searcher for *inter-rater reliability calculator* never types "ReviewVahti". | Keep the product name in the title tag (it's there); make the H1 the job, e.g. ReviewVahti → `Compute screening reliability you can report.` — the title/description already carry κ, PABAK, AC1, Krippendorff's α for the query match. |
| `extractvahti/` | Finding #1: title jargon-first ("DTA"), truncating description. | As §1.1. |
| `agents/` | Strong page, but the query it can own — *llms.txt example site* / *AI-agent-ready website* — isn't in title or H1. Niche but growing. | Optional: fold "llms.txt" into body copy prominently (it is the living example). |

**Would these pages deserve to rank above universities/NIH?** For the
problem-shaped queries (sample size justification, reviewer response, predatory
journal, saturation): **yes on content** — they answer immediately, show worked
examples, and cite primary literature. What they lack against .edu/.gov
competitors is (a) inbound authority — outside SEO scope per your brief, but note
internal linking §8 is the controllable part — and (b) visible
publication/updated dates, which both users and quality raters use to judge
currency (§4/§6).

## Phase 4 — Helpful content review

Assessed against Google's self-assessment questions
([Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

**Genuinely strong (keep; this is the moat):**
- **Original evidence:** `learn/local-models-check-citations/` reports the site's
  own 120 claim-source-pair test; `learn/transcribe-interviews-locally/` prints an
  honest error-rate table from the author's own Finland-Swedish interviews
  including the finding that hand transcription was faster — content that
  *against-interest* honesty makes almost unfakeable.
- **First-hand expertise:** visible MD/PhD byline on articles; supervision
  articles written from actual supervisor experience.
- **No AI-filler tells:** descriptions are concrete (named standards, numbers,
  artifacts) per the house standard; no "in today's fast-paced world" class of
  filler found.
- **Anti-overclaim invariant** ("agreement is not accuracy", "a result about the
  instrument, not the study") — reads as expertise, not modesty.

**Gaps:**

1. **23 of 41 learn articles have `datePublished` in JSON-LD but no visible date**
   (`<time>` present on only 18). Google's guidance on dates
   ([Provide a publish date](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates))
   asks for a visible date consistent with structured data. Add a byline row
   (`Heidi Andersén, MD, PhD · Updated <date>`) to the article template.
2. **Merge/pair candidates:** `manuscript-readiness-check/` + `lahtotilannetesti/`
   are translations — pair with hreflang, don't merge. `ai-use-disclosure-statement/`
   (how to write one) and `ai-use-disclosure-example/` (copy-paste sentences) serve
   distinct intents — keep both but cross-link each in the first paragraph, and
   make the Kit page (`ai-disclosure-kit/`) the commercial parent both link up to.
3. **Cornerstone candidates:** the qualitative cluster (~15 articles) has no
   cornerstone. Build `learn/qualitative-research/` as a hub (see §5) rather than
   a new mega-article — the parts already exist.
4. **Promotional balance:** product pages are appropriately promotional; learn
   articles keep tool mentions restrained (correct). No page reads as made-for-search.

## Phase 5 — Information architecture

Current state: **products at root** (`/citevahti/` …), **problems in `/learn/`**,
plus two proto-workflow pages that already think in researcher tasks:
`/download/` ("by research stage") and `/learn/` ("What are you stuck on right
now?"). The architecture is *half-migrated* to task-orientation — finish it.

**Recommended architecture (evolution, not rebuild):**

```
Home
├── Workflow hubs (new thin hub pages, each ~1 screen + links)
│   ├── /research-question/    → turn-reading-into-a-question, find-your-next-research-project, Research Strategy Kit
│   ├── /study-design/         → StudyVahti, RecoverLite, MethodVahti, defend-qualitative-sample-size
│   ├── /data-collection/      → DictVahti, data-dictionary, survey-export, decimal-comma, clean-and-prepare
│   ├── /analysis/             → QualiVahti, codes-to-themes, saturation pair, read-your-own-statistics, EpiNet
│   ├── /writing/              → Manuscript Kit, how-to-write, agile-writing, figures, limitations (gap §9)
│   ├── /submission/           → reference-check, is-this-journal-predatory, AI disclosure trio
│   ├── /peer-review/          → ReviewVahti*, Reviewer's Notebook, how-to-peer-review, respond-to-reviewers, triage
│   ├── /evidence-synthesis/   → CiteVahti, ExtractVahti, SynthVahti, GuidelineVahti, systematic-review-when-to-stop
│   └── /research-integrity/   → AuditLite, document-ai-assisted-analysis, cite-your-analysis-code, open-source
```

Practical notes: (a) `/download/` already *is* this page in embryo — either
promote its sections into hubs or make it the single "workflow" page and add
in-page anchors; don't build both. (b) Learn articles already carry visible
breadcrumbs (`nav.voc-crumbs`) — extend the same crumb to product pages
(`Home › Evidence synthesis › CiteVahti`) once hubs exist, and back it with
`BreadcrumbList` schema (§7). (c) Keep URLs stable — hubs are *additive*; no
migrations, no redirects needed.

## Phase 8 — Internal linking

Graph facts: every indexable page reachable in ≤4 clicks; no orphans. The weak
tail — **15 indexable pages with ≤2 inbound links** — includes exactly the pages
that should be cluster members:

`open-source/`, `citevahti/privacy/`, `guidelinevahti/pilot/sample/`, and learn
articles: `ai-use-disclosure-example`, `clean-and-prepare-your-data`,
`codes-to-themes`, `figures-too-low-resolution`, `is-this-journal-predatory`,
`lessons-from-being-a-supervisor`, `llms-in-qualitative-research`,
`minimal-model-qualitative-text`, `obsidian-plugins-for-researchers`,
`recover-corrupted-bibliography`, `should-peer-reviewers-be-paid`,
`turn-reading-into-a-question`.

Strategy (three passes, all additive):

1. **Complete the intra-cluster mesh.** Learn articles have a `nav.voc-related`
   block — audit it per cluster so every member links 2–4 siblings
   (qualitative cluster; publishing/peer-review cluster; Obsidian cluster; data
   cluster; AI-use cluster). This alone lifts most of the ≤2-inbound tail.
2. **Product ↔ guide reciprocity.** Every tool page links its supporting guides
   in body copy (most do); every guide links its tool once, in context, not a
   banner (e.g. `codes-to-themes` → QualiVahti; `is-this-journal-predatory` →
   reference-check). Check the 15 above specifically.
3. **Hub wiring (§5).** Once workflow hubs exist, each hub links every member and
   every member crumbs back — this is what converts 41 good articles into
   topical authority Google can see as a cluster rather than a list.
