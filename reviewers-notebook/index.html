Comment eight may be a flaw. Or it may be your paper, not theirs.

The hard part of peer review is not finding more things to criticise. It is deciding which findings matter, which can be fixed, and which should never become demands.

A fair review is specific enough for the authors to act on and clear enough for the editor to understand your recommendation.

One path from reading to recommendation

01

Reconstruct the paper

Name the paper type, central claim, study, analysis, and numbers before raising points.

02

Locate the problem

Map each criticism to a sentence, table, figure, or method choice.

03

Classify it

Separate a fatal flaw, major fix, minor issue, editorial suggestion, preference, and discipline mismatch.

04

Test the demand

Check whether you are asking for impossible new data, the wrong standard, or simply your preferred design.

05

Write both notes

Export the author-facing review and a separate confidential note for the editor.

A review point has somewhere to go

The Notebook does not reward a longer list. It helps you decide what to ask for, what to offer as optional, and what to drop.

ASKA real flaw or missing report

State what is wrong, where it is, and what would make the paper assessable or defensible.

OFFERA useful editorial suggestion

Mark it as optional. Do not make publication depend on your preferred wording or citation.

DROPA preference dressed as a rule

Keep it out of the review when it does not change whether the claim can stand.

Would you sign it?

Every retained point passes seven integrity questions, including whether it maps to the paper and whether you would put your name beside it.

The tool structures your judgement.It does not read the manuscript, decide whether a claim is true, or make the recommendation for you.

Practical questions

Does the tool review the paper for me?

No. It helps you reconstruct the paper, classify your own points, spot reviewer traps, and assemble your words. The judgement and recommendation remain yours.

Will it work offline?

Yes. The Review Triage tool is one HTML file that opens in a browser without an account or internet connection. The other files are PDF and Markdown.

Is it only for medical research?

No. The worked example is clinical, but the paper-type routing and decision method cover quantitative, qualitative, diagnostic, prediction, and evidence-synthesis work.
