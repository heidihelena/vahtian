Justify your sample size — honestly.

MethodVahti turns your qualitative research-design parameters into a COREQ/SRQR-compatible methods PDF: three independent sample-size models, a hierarchical heterogeneity score, and an evidence-graded audit trail you can paste into a manuscript. It never hands you one number and calls it the truth — you decide N; the tool documents the decision.

How it works Six steps. You confirm N before any PDF is generated.

  1. Elicit. Research question, orientation, collection & analysis strategy, reflexivity — the COREQ items, captured once.
  2. Heterogeneity. If you have coded pilot records, qualitative_heterogeneity_score() estimates how varied your design is across eight dimensions.
  3. Optimise. Three models — linear saturation, network complexity, fuzzy-set QCA — each propose an N, adjusted for information power.
  4. Confirm. You see all three estimates and a stability range, then state your chosen N and stopping criterion in your own words.
  5. Generate. A COREQ/SRQR-compatible PDF: study profile, optimisation, sensitivity, limitations, the full 32-item checklist, and the audit record.
  6. Submit. The PDF is the deliverable — a defensible answer when a reviewer asks why your sample is the size it is.

Three scores, never one Qualitative heterogeneity is not one natural number. We don't pretend it is.

Primary ○

Hierarchical score

Heterogeneity estimated within each dimension, then aggregated across them. This is the number that feeds sample-size optimisation.

Diagnostic ◆

Marginal map

A descriptive, per-dimension breakdown — which dimensions actually carry the variation, as a plain unweighted mean.

Diagnostic ○

Sparse-interaction stress

The full cross-tabulation with a sparsity penalty, so data gaps are visible — never silently driving the result.

Severity weight ≠ observed rate. An outcome weight of 0.90 means near-maximum amplification severity, not "90% disagreement". Defaults are author hypotheses; teams audit them with pilot data, and every change is timestamped in the report.

Open validation, in progress

We don't hide that the primary heterogeneity score is an author hypothesis (○) — Vahtian's construction, not externally validated. We are testing it against open-access qualitative datasets and published meta-syntheses, and we will publish the validation protocol and results openly. Until that work lands, treat the score as decision support, not ground truth.

Honest limits: MethodVahti does not infer causality, does not validate study quality, and does not replace researcher judgment. The number is a synthesis you defend — not a verdict it issues.
